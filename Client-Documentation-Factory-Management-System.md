# Factory Management System  
## Client Documentation — Furniture Factory

**Document purpose:** This overview explains what the system does, how your team will use it day to day, and how it supports better control of orders, money, and profitability.

---

## 1. Project Overview

The **Factory Management System** is a single place for your furniture factory to run core business operations from order to cash.

Instead of juggling notebooks, spreadsheets, and memory, your team can:

- **Manage orders** from quote to production and delivery, including custom pieces typical of furniture work.  
- **Track deliveries** accurately when jobs are shipped in more than one go.  
- **Follow payments** when customers pay in installments, so you always know what is paid and what is still due.  
- **Record expenses** such as materials, labor-related costs, and daily factory spending.  
- **See financial clarity** through reports that show income, costs, and profit over the periods you care about.

The system is designed around how furniture businesses actually work: custom products, staged deliveries, and flexible payment terms.

---

## 2. Key Features

### Product creation during order

Furniture is often made to order or highly customized. When creating an order, staff can **define a new product** (description, specifications, pricing as you define them) or **pick an existing product** from your catalog. This keeps every order tied to clear line items without forcing you to maintain products that you never repeat.

### Order management

Users create orders with **customer details**, **line items** (products, quantities, and prices), and **delivery expectations** (timeline or target dates as you use them in your process). Orders become the central record for production, delivery, and payment activity.

### Partial delivery system

Large or multi-item orders do not always leave the factory in one shipment. For example, if a customer orders **10 doors**, you might deliver **3 first** and the **remaining 7 later**.

The system tracks:

- **How much has been delivered** per line item.  
- **How much is still to deliver.**

This reduces confusion about what left the factory and what is still pending.

### Partial payment tracking

Customers often pay **in stages** (deposit, progress, balance). The system records each payment against the order and shows:

- **Total paid to date**  
- **Amount still due**

You get a clear picture of cash received versus outstanding obligations, without manual reconciliation on paper.

### Expense and cost tracking

Authorized users can log **daily factory expenses** and **production-related costs** (for example materials, outsourced work, or other costs you choose to track). This builds a foundation for understanding where money goes, not only what comes in from sales.

### Financial reports

Reports summarize **income**, **expenses**, and **profit** so leadership can see performance at a glance. Typical ways to view the numbers include:

- Today  
- Yesterday  
- This week  
- This month  
- This year  
- **Custom date range** (choose any start and end dates)

This supports both routine checks and deeper reviews for a specific project or period.

### Role-based access control

Not everyone needs the same level of access. The system supports **different roles** (such as **Admin**, **Manager**, and **Staff**) with **permissions matched to responsibility**:

- Day-to-day staff can work on orders, deliveries, or payments as you allow.  
- Managers can oversee operations and costs.  
- Admins can manage users, sensitive settings, and full reporting as you define.

This keeps data orderly and limits who can change critical information.

### Printable documents

The application supports **printing** key paperwork directly from the system, including:

- **Invoices**  
- **Payment receipts**  
- **Reports** (as designed for your workflow)

This helps when you need a paper copy for the customer, accounting, or internal files.

## 3. User Journey

The following is a typical end-to-end flow your team might follow:

1. **Sign in**  
   The user opens the system in a web browser and logs in with their account.

2. **Customer**  
   The user **creates a new customer** or **selects an existing one** so the order is linked to the right party.

3. **Order and products**  
   The user **creates an order**, adds line items, and for each item either **creates a new product** (custom) or **selects an existing product**, with quantities, prices, and delivery timing as agreed.

4. **Production**  
   The factory **produces** according to the order. The system holds the order as the reference for what was promised.

5. **Partial deliveries**  
   As goods are ready to go out, the user **records each delivery**, including partial quantities, so delivered and remaining amounts stay accurate.

6. **Partial payments**  
   As money arrives, the user **records payments** against the order. The system updates paid and due amounts automatically from these entries.

7. **Expenses**  
   A **manager** (or role you assign) **records daily expenses** and relevant production costs so spending is captured in one place.

8. **Reports**  
   An **admin** or **manager** reviews **dashboards and financial reports** (by day, week, month, year, or custom dates) to monitor income, expenses, and profit.

9. **Printing**  
   When needed, the user **prints invoices, payment receipts, or reports** for customers or internal use.

This journey can be adapted to your internal roles: the same person might do several steps, or different people might own deliveries versus payments, depending on how you organize the factory.

## 4. Benefits for the Factory

| Benefit | What it means for you |
|--------|------------------------|
| **Better order tracking** | Every order lives in one record with products, deliveries, and payments linked to it—easier to answer “Where is this job?” and “What’s left?” |
| **Clear payment management** | Installments and balances are visible on the order, reducing disputes and missed follow-ups on overdue amounts. |
| **Expense control** | Spending is logged systematically, so you can spot patterns and compare costs to revenue over time. |
| **Profit visibility** | Income, expenses, and profit in filtered reports support better decisions without rebuilding spreadsheets each month. |
| **Less manual work** | Fewer separate lists and manual calculations; the system carries quantities, amounts, and summaries forward as your team records real activity. |

Together, these outcomes support a more professional operation and clearer communication with customers and owners.

## 5. System Type

The Factory Management System is delivered as a **web application**.

- **Access:** Your team uses it through a **web browser**—no separate installation on each computer for typical use.  
- **Devices:** It is intended to work on **desktop and laptop** browsers, and on **mobile browsers** when you need to check or enter information away from the desk (subject to screen size and your internal policies).

Hosting, security, and backup arrangements can be aligned with your IT or vendor setup; this document focuses on **what the system does for the business**, not on technical infrastructure.
